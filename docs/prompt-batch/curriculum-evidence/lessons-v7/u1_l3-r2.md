```text
So why does this happen?

---

**Hallucination** is when an AI generates text that sounds authoritative but is factually false — made-up names, invented studies, events that never occurred.

It's not a bug in the usual sense. It's a direct consequence of how these models work.

---

Remember next-token prediction? The model is always asking: *what word comes next, given everything before it?*

It's optimized to produce text that *sounds right* — fluent, confident, well-structured. Truth isn't part of that equation.

[KEY]LLMs are built to generate plausible text, not accurate text — and plausible often looks identical to accurate.[/KEY]

[EX]In *Mata v. Avianca* (2023), lawyers used ChatGPT to research cases. It produced citations with real-looking case names, docket numbers, and quotes — all fabricated. The lawyers filed the brief in federal court. A judge caught it.[/EX]

---
```

### TUTOR turn 1 (concise; out=402 tok; $0.0117)

```text
Exactly right — "makes stuff up" is actually a pretty precise description.

The model isn't retrieving facts from a database. It's completing a pattern. A citation *looks like* author names, a case title, a year, a court.

So it generates all those pieces fluently — whether or not any of it is real.

---

Now here's the second piece, and it's a little counterintuitive.

You might expect an AI to sound *less* sure when it's wrong. But the opposite often happens.

**Calibration** is the match between how confident a system sounds and how often it's actually correct.

A well-calibrated system sounds uncertain when it's guessing. A poorly calibrated one sounds equally sure whether it's right or wrong.

---

OpenAI's GPT-4 Technical Report (2023) found something striking: the base model's confidence actually matched its accuracy reasonably well — but after post-training (the RLHF process that turns it into a polished assistant), that calibration got *worse*.

Making the model sound more helpful and fluent made it sound more confident across the board — even when it shouldn't be.

[KEY]Post-training makes AI sound more polished and assured — which can mask the moments when it's completely wrong.[/KEY]

[EX]A lawyer reading ChatGPT's fake citations had no signal that anything was off. The prose was smooth, the format was correct, the confidence was total.[/EX]

---
```
