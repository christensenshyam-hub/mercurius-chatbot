```text
Here's something that might surprise you: the word "bank" — whether you mean a riverbank or a place to store money — becomes the **same token** every single time.

The model doesn't know which you mean until it does something more sophisticated. That's what makes this interesting.

So: what even is a token?

When you hit send, your prompt doesn't travel as words. It gets sliced into chunks called **tokens** — roughly syllables or short words — and each token is converted to a number.

[KEY]Tokenization turns your text into a sequence of numbers before the model sees a single word.[/KEY]

[EX]GPT-4's tokenizer knows about 100,000 different tokens. "unbelievable" might become three tokens: "un", "believ", "able" — each mapped to its own number.[/EX]

Those numbers are the only thing the model ever actually reads.
```

```text
Exactly right — tokens are just numbered chunks, no fact-lookup involved.

Now here's where it gets clever. Remember how "bank" gets the same token whether you mean a river or a savings account?

The model sorts that out using something called the **attention mechanism**.

When the model processes your tokens, it doesn't treat each one in isolation. It weighs every token against every other token in your prompt — asking, in effect, "which other words should I pay attention to right now?"

[KEY]Attention lets the model figure out what each token *means* by looking at the tokens surrounding it.[/KEY]

[EX]In "I went to the bank to deposit money," attention links "bank" strongly to "deposit" and "money" — so the model understands it's a financial institution, not a riverbank.[/EX]

This is how context shapes meaning, all from numbers.

---
```

```text
Now for the third piece: what does the model actually *do* after attention sorts out the meaning?

It predicts the next token. That's genuinely all it does — one token at a time, over and over.

After processing your prompt through attention, the model produces a probability score for every token in its vocabulary. The highest-scoring token gets picked, appended to the sequence, and then the whole thing runs again.

[KEY]An LLM generates text by repeatedly predicting the single most likely next token, given everything before it.[/KEY]

[EX]Given "The cat sat on the ___", nearly every probability mass lands on "mat" — so the model picks it with high confidence. But given "My favorite color is ___", hundreds of tokens score similarly, so confidence is low and the output feels more random.[/EX]

---
```
