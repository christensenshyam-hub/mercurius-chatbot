```text
Here's something that might surprise you: two people can ask an AI the *exact same thing* and get completely different answers — not because the AI is random, but because of how they phrased it.

The AI isn't reading your mind. It's reading your words.

[KEY]The frame you put around a request shapes everything: the AI's assumed role, its audience, its tone, and even what it thinks you want.[/KEY]

Let's start with the first big idea: **specificity vs. vagueness**.

A vague prompt gives the AI too much room. It picks defaults — usually generic, surface-level, aimed at nobody in particular.

[EX]"Tell me about climate change" → a textbook overview, 5 paragraphs, no particular angle. "Explain the three biggest obstacles to cutting carbon emissions, in plain English, for a 10th grader who's skeptical about the urgency" → focused, audience-aware, structured.[/EX]

Same topic. Completely different outputs — because the second prompt told the AI *who it's talking to*, *what shape the answer should take*, and *what angle to take*.
```

### TUTOR turn 1 (balanced; out=339 tok; $0.0106)

```text
Not quite — AI models don't get confused and quit.

When details are missing, the model fills the gaps with whatever is most statistically common for that kind of request. It doesn't struggle; it just defaults to the most average, generic version of an answer.

Think of it like ordering at a restaurant by saying "I'll have food." The kitchen doesn't freeze up — they just bring you whatever the chef decides is a safe bet.

Now let's add the second big idea: **role prompting**.

You can tell the AI to *be* someone — and that shifts its entire perspective, vocabulary, and priorities.

[EX]"What are the risks of this business plan?" → a balanced, neutral list. "You are a skeptical venture capitalist who has seen 500 startups fail. What are the risks of this business plan?" → sharper, more specific, focused on the failure modes that actually kill companies.[/EX]

Same question. But the role gives the AI a lens — and that lens changes what it notices and how it talks.

[KEY]Assigning a role to the AI isn't a gimmick — it's a way of telling it whose expertise and perspective to channel.[/KEY]
```

```text
You got it — the role changes the *perspective* the AI writes from, not some hidden setting or data source.

Now the third idea, and then we'll put it all together.

**The "garbage in, garbage out" principle** — applied to prompts.

The AI can only work with what you give it. If your prompt has a wrong assumption baked in, the AI will usually just go along with it rather than push back.

[EX]"Why is coffee bad for teenagers?" assumes coffee IS bad — so the AI writes a list of harms. "What does the research actually say about coffee and teenagers?" leaves room for a real answer, which is more complicated.[/EX]

A loaded frame produces a loaded answer. The AI isn't fact-checking your premise — it's completing your thought.

[KEY]Your prompt's hidden assumptions become the AI's starting point — so a flawed frame produces a confidently flawed answer.[/KEY]

Now let's put this to work.

**Here's your exercise:**
```
