```text
Here's something that might surprise you: two people can ask an AI the exact same *question* and get completely different *answers* — not because the AI is random, but because the framing itself is part of the input.

The AI doesn't read your mind. It reads your words.

[KEY]How you frame a prompt — the role, the specificity, the context — shapes the output as much as the topic itself.[/KEY]

This is the first idea: **framing**.

When you change the frame around a question, you change what the AI thinks you want, who it thinks you are, and what kind of answer would satisfy you.

[EX]Ask "What is photosynthesis?" and you get a textbook definition. Ask "Explain photosynthesis like I'm a 10-year-old who loves Minecraft" and you get a completely different register — probably something about plants "crafting" sunlight into food. Ask "You are a biology professor writing a grant proposal. Describe the significance of photosynthesis" and the AI shifts into formal, persuasive academic prose. Same topic. Three totally different outputs.[/EX]

Notice what changed each time: the assumed audience, the assigned role, and the level of specificity.
```

```text
Exactly right — "chef-y" is actually a perfect way to put it.

Now there's a second idea worth adding: **specificity**.

Vague prompts get vague answers. The more detail you give — audience, purpose, format, constraints — the more the AI can narrow down which response actually fits your need.

[EX]"Write me something about climate change" could produce a poem, a news article, a five-paragraph essay, or a bullet-point summary. The AI has to guess. But "Write a 3-sentence explanation of climate change for a middle-school science fair poster, using no jargon" leaves almost nothing to guess — you get exactly what you described.[/EX]

Framing (role, audience) and specificity (format, length, constraints) work together. They're both ways of narrowing the prediction toward what you actually want.

[KEY]Specificity reduces the AI's guessing — every detail you add rules out outputs you didn't want.[/KEY]
```
