```text
Correct — and that's actually a bigger deal than it sounds.

If the *input* (facial expressions) doesn't reliably predict the *output* (job success), then the score the AI produces is essentially noise — but it still gets used to filter real people out of real jobs.

That brings us to the third piece: what happens *after* these tools are deployed, and who's watching them.

Illinois passed the **AI Video Interview Act** in January 2020 — the first law of its kind in the US. It requires employers to tell applicants that AI will analyze their video, explain how it works, get their consent, and delete the footage on request.

New York City went further with **Local Law 144**, enforced since July 2023. Any automated hiring tool used there must get a yearly independent bias audit, with a public summary anyone can read.

These laws exist because without them, candidates often have no idea an AI even scored them.

[KEY]Disclosure and audit laws are the main check on AI hiring tools — without them, candidates may never know an algorithm rejected them.[/KEY]

[EX]New York City's Local Law 144 requires a yearly independent bias audit for automated hiring tools, with results posted publicly — so job seekers can actually see how the tool performs across demographic groups.[/EX]
```
