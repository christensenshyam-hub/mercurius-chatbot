==================== run 1 alg-explain-more [socratic]

--- USER: [Explain more chip, responseMode deep]
The key idea is **inverse operations** — every operation has an opposite that undoes it.

Addition and subtraction undo each other. Multiplication and division undo each other.

In 3x + 5 = 20, the 5 is *added* to 3x. So the inverse is subtracting 5 from both sides — that's how you isolate the 3x.

Why both sides? Because an equation is a balance: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other to keep it equal.

So: what do you get when you subtract 5 from both sides?
   [flags] 2 questions

--- USER: [Explain more chip, responseMode deep]
Here's why "both sides" is more than a rule to memorize.

An equation is a statement that two things are *equal* — like a perfectly balanced scale. The "=" sign is the fulcrum.

When you subtract 5 from the left side only, you're taking weight off one pan. The scale tips. The statement is no longer true.

Subtracting 5 from *both* sides removes the same weight from both pans simultaneously — the balance is preserved, and the equation stays true.

This is called the **properties of equality**: whatever legal operation you apply to one side, applying it identically to the other keeps the equation valid.

"Legal" matters here. You can add, subtract, multiply, or divide — but dividing by zero is illegal because it breaks math entirely (0 has no multiplicative inverse).

Now try yours: subtract 5 from both sides of 3x + 5 = 20. What's the new equation?

==================== run 2 alg-explain-more [socratic] 

--- USER: what is 3x+5 = 20
What's your first move to get the 3x by itself?

--- USER: [Explain more chip, responseMode deep]
The big idea here is **inverse operations** — every operation has an "undo."

Addition and subtraction undo each other. Multiplication and division undo each other.

Your equation has two things "on top of" the x: a +5 and a ×3. To free x, you peel them off in reverse order — last thing on, first thing off.

The +5 went on last, so you undo it first. What operation undoes adding 5?

--- USER: [Explain more chip, responseMode deep]
Here's why the order matters — and why it's not arbitrary.

When you wrote 3x + 5, two operations happened to x in sequence:

1. x got **multiplied by 3** → giving you 3x
2. Then 5 got **added** → giving you 3x + 5

To reverse a sequence, you always work backwards. Think of putting on shoes and socks: socks first, then shoes. To undo it: shoes off first, then socks.

Same logic.

If you tried to divide by 3 first (before subtracting 5), you'd get (3x + 5) ÷ 3, which splits into x + 5/3 — now you've got a fraction tangled in, and the algebra gets messier than it needs to be.

Subtract 5 first, and you get a clean 3x = 15. Then dividing by 3 is one smooth step.

So the rule is: **undo addition/subtraction before multiplication/division** when you're isolating a variable.

Now — back to your problem. You've got 3x + 5 = 20. What do you get when you subtract 5 from both sides?
