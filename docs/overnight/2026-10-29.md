## Round 5: a fresh-eyes review of each stack as a whole ($0)

Four reviewers each read one whole stack (server correctness, privacy and Postgres, iPhone, lessons). A skeptic then tried to disprove each finding. **Seven findings were confirmed; all were low severity once checked.**

**One of them was a privacy regression introduced tonight, in #78. Round 6 fixed it in #78 itself.**

- **#78's new network rows:** after "Delete my data", the network's new-session and report rows survive. They carry the exact millisecond the erased session started and filed its report, next to the network hash, and are kept 400 days. Only today's count is needed. The privacy page still says erasure removes usage rows.
- **#78, a failed retry:** a "Delete my data" retry that fails wipes the first erasure's note, so a photo or usage row still being written is kept under the erased id.
- **Midnight read-back (since #75):** a ledger read-back running at midnight seeds the new day with the old day's counts. For a network, that could block a school until the next midnight. It is rare: the first read after a late-night deploy has to straddle midnight.
- **Quiz me (#67):** if the first ask is refused and the retry succeeds, the quiz costs no turn. Dollar caps still apply.
- **Erasure plus a restart:** gives a network back the dollars and photos its erased sessions used. This isn't worse than main, where every restart gave a fresh day. Keeping that count would mean keeping more data, so this is your call.
- **iPhone, Stop or New Chat during a photo upload:** the cancelled upload overwrites the screen. Stop loses its Retry, and the new chat shows as failed. This is already true in 2.3.0.
- **iPhone, after a photo-limit bounce (#77):** "Explain more" and the practice chip disappear until the next send.
- **Lessons (#69):** "correct option first, as A" relies on the server shuffling the options. When the shuffle skips a check because an option seems to mention another, A is always right.

Checked and sound:
- Postgres schema changes are safe on a live table.
- No raw IP or session id lands in the new rows, logs or alerts.
- Read-backs never block a request when Postgres is down.
- The safety block is still last in every assembled prompt.
